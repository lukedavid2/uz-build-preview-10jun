Why window rhymes the way it does

Pull window apart phonetically and you get a two-syllable word with the open /ɒ/ (/ɒ/) as the rhyme-bearing vowel; the close doesn't really close at all. In our 54,000-word dictionary the perfect-rhyme pool returns 0 matches, family rhymes 0, additive and subtractive together 91, assonance 13,152, and consonance 511. That's a narrow strict column with a very deep slant well beneath it. Modern songwriting reads those slants as rhymes; the ear has been trained on them for a century. In practice: start at the top of the perfect column, scan family next, and reach for the assonance pool when the strict matches feel worn. A lyric that uses only strict rhymes for window tends to read as dated; the contemporary ear forgives — and often prefers — the slant.
