On the page, money is a workaday word; on the ear it's a two-syllable word on the front /ɛ/ that doesn't close on a consonant at all. The lyric earns it by placement, not by selection. When the search is rhymes for money, the answer takes a specific form: perfect-rhyme territory is narrow, the family-rhyme list contributes ear-friendly slants, while the assonance well runs into four figures. Its lyric role is a low-register anchor. When the strict column thins, the family column is the most singable next step.

Why money rhymes the way it does

The rhyme map for money starts at the vowel — the centred /ɛ/, IPA /ə/ — and ends where the line leaves the vowel hanging open. In our 54,000-word dictionary the perfect-rhyme pool returns 8 matches, family rhymes 8, additive and subtractive together 6, assonance 4,261, and consonance 1046. The mix gives you options across the board. Strict rhymes for the structural beats, family or assonance for the interior lines. What matters when you're writing: the ear forgives slants in service of meaning. If the strict rhyme is the predictable word, the assonance match will usually hit harder. Money is a word where the slant choice almost always reads as more thoughtful than the obvious end-rhyme.
