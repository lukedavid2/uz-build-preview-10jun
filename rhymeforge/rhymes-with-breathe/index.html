Why breathe rhymes the way it does

Breathe is one-syllable, its rhyme-relevant vowel sitting on the long /iː/, then it lets the line dissolve into a fricative. In our 54,000-word dictionary the perfect-rhyme pool returns 11 matches, family rhymes 0, additive and subtractive together 144, assonance 3,222, and consonance 129. The empty family column matters less than it looks. Family rhymes are a strict-classifier construct; the songwriter's ear accepts most assonance matches in their place. What matters when you're writing: the ear forgives slants in service of meaning. If the strict rhyme is the predictable word, the assonance match will usually hit harder. Breathe is a word where the slant choice almost always reads as more thoughtful than the obvious end-rhyme.
