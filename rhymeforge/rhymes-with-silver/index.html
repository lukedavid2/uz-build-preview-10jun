Why silver rhymes the way it does

The rhyme map for silver starts at the vowel — the rhotic schwa, IPA /ɜːr/ — and ends where the line flows into the next line via a liquid. In our 54,000-word dictionary the perfect-rhyme pool returns 2 matches, family rhymes 0, additive and subtractive together 60, assonance 9,435, and consonance 31. That's a narrow strict column with a very deep slant well beneath it. Modern songwriting reads those slants as rhymes; the ear has been trained on them for a century. Writing-tip: don't end every line with the strict rhyme. Use the slant columns at the joints and the strict matches at the seams. Silver works hardest when the slant carries the verse and the strict match closes the chorus.
