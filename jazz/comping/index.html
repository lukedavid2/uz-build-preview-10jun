Comping — the actual job▶ Spotify▶ YouTube

"Comping" — accompanying — is what you do for nine bars out of ten, and it has two halves the lesson sites too often split up: which notes (voicings) and when (rhythm). Half an hour on each and you can play with other people; everything after that is refinement.

Learn this page in three steps
  1. Learn the two shell grips and comp a ii–V–I from memory.
  2. Play the Charleston for one full chorus — placement over variety.
  3. Then mix: two bars Charleston, one anticipation, one bar out.

Half one: the voicings

Shells — the 80% solution

Root, 3rd, 7th. The 3rd and 7th define the chord's quality and contain all the voice-leading motion; the root can go entirely once a bassist owns it. Two grips cover a whole ii–V–I, and they sit under two fingers:

Drop-2 — the full-dress voicings

Take a four-note chord, drop its second-highest note an octave, and it falls perfectly onto four adjacent guitar strings. Drop-2 chords on the top four strings are the sound of Wes's block-chord choruses and most comping you've ever admired. Learn one chord quality at a time, all four inversions up the neck:

The practice that compounds: play a ii–V–I using only drop-2 voicings, choosing each next chord by smallest possible movement. That constraint teaches voice leading faster than any theory page.

The etiquette (what separates accompanists from obstacles)

  • Listen louder than you play. Comp in the holes of the melody, never on top of it — same law as blues fills.
  • Don't loop one figure. Two bars of Charleston, then vary it — displacement is interest.
  • Trade registers. If the piano comps high, you go low or lay out. Two compers in one register is mud.
  • Laying out is comping. The bravest accompaniment on half the great records is silence behind the first chorus.

Before you move on, you should be able to…
  • ☐ Comp a ii–V–I with shells, eyes closed, in two keys.
  • ☐ Hold the Charleston for a chorus without drifting.
  • ☐ Play one chorus where you lay out for four bars — and mean it.
Tie-in: loop a ii–V–I in the builder and practise each rhythm figure against it — the loop holds the harmony so your whole attention goes to placement.
